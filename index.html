District 1 & beyond

Things to do

Most of these are within 15 minutes of the apartment.

Bùi Viện walking street 7 min taxi

The backpacker nightlife strip: loud, neon, cheap beer, pedestrianised in the evenings. About 25 minutes on foot if you'd rather walk it, or a quick taxi.

Saigon Skydeck 12 min

Bitexco Tower, 49th floor. Not the city's tallest deck, but the best positioned: you look straight down onto Ben Thanh Market, the Saigon River bend and the old colonial quarter. Go around 5:30pm for sunset into city lights. Tickets around 240,000đ, or skip the fee entirely by having a drink at EON Heli Bar on floor 52.

Independence Palace 11 min

The site where the war ended in 1975, tanks and all. 1960s architecture preserved almost untouched, including the underground command bunker. Ticket counter 8am to 3:30pm, palace closes 4:30 to 5pm. Around 40,000đ, or 65,000đ with the exhibition. Allow 1.5 to 2 hours and go in the morning to beat the heat.

Saigon Central Post Office Free · 11 min

French colonial post office from 1891, still a working post office rather than a museum piece. High vaulted hall, old hand painted maps on the walls, an easy 15 minute stop. Open 7:30am to 6pm, Sunday until 5pm. Buy a postcard at the counter and send it home; it genuinely still works. Early morning or just before closing avoids the tour groups.

Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica Free · 11 min

Red brick French Gothic landmark from the 1880s, directly across from the Post Office. Under restoration until around 2027, so interior access is limited, but the square and exterior are fully open. Best light before 9am or after 4pm. Sunday 9:30am Mass is held in English.

War Remnants Museum 12 min

28 Võ Văn Tần, District 3. Photographs, military hardware and personal artefacts documenting the Vietnam War from the Vietnamese side. Powerful and heavy, the Agent Orange exhibit especially, so it is not for everyone, but widely considered essential. Open 7:30am to 5:30pm, last entry 4pm, around 40,000đ. Budget 1.5 to 2 hours.

Ben Thanh Market 7 min

The city's oldest and most central market: souvenirs, clothing, produce and a food court with cheap, solid Vietnamese eating. Open 6am to 6pm, with street food stalls outside running late. Bargain hard, start at about half the asking price. Busiest and hottest 10am to 3pm.

Fine Arts Museum 10 min

97A Phó Đức Chính, one of the closest attractions to the apartment. A wealthy Chinese-Vietnamese family's 1929 mansion turned art museum, and the yellow and green colonial building is honestly the main event. Champa sculpture through to modern lacquer painting. No air conditioning, so go morning or late afternoon. Daily 8am to 5pm, around 30,000đ.

Ho Chi Minh City Museum 10 min

65 Lý Tự Trọng, the former Gia Long Palace. French neoclassical building covering the city's history from the colonial era through reunification, with a preserved wartime bunker underneath. Quieter and far less touristed than Independence Palace. Daily 8am to 5pm, around 30,000đ, about an hour.

Saigon Opera House Exterior · 7 min

7 Lam Sơn Square. French colonial theatre from 1900, built to echo the Petit Palais in Paris, and one of the city's most photographed buildings, especially lit up at night. No general public access; the interior is only seen by attending a performance. The AO Show, acrobatics set to Vietnamese village life, runs most evenings from around 800,000đ.

Jade Emperor Pagoda Free · 15 min

73 Mai Thị Lựu, Đa Kao. A 1909 Taoist temple thick with incense smoke, intricate carvings and a turtle pond out the back; a completely different atmosphere from the French colonial buildings elsewhere on this list. Open around 7am to 6pm, free, donations welcome. Dress modestly, shoulders and knees covered.

Cu Chi Tunnels Half day tour

A network of Viet Cong wartime tunnels about 1.5 to 2 hours outside the city. Best done as a half day tour with hotel pickup, around $15 to $40 USD with entry included. Book through Klook, Viator or GetYourGuide rather than turning up independently. Pairs naturally with the War Remnants Museum, though that is a lot of heavy history for one day.

Good to know First time here?
  • Grab is the default for taxis and food delivery. Download it before you arrive.
  • Cash still rules at street stalls and markets. Cards work in malls, supermarkets and most restaurants.
  • Tap water is not drinkable. Bottled water is cheap everywhere, and there is drinking water in the apartment.
  • Crossing the road: walk slowly and predictably and the traffic flows around you. Don't stop or run.
  • Keep your phone in your hand, not out over the road. Phone snatching from passing bikes is the one common street crime here.
  • Emergency numbers: police 113, fire 114, ambulance 115.
